---
title: Item count assertions
description: Assert that an exporter is reached, receives exactly N items, or never receives anything.
icon: Hash
---

Exporter assertions check delivery. They name an exporter ID from your config:

```yaml
expect:
  - exporter: otlp_http/acme        # at least one item
  - exporter: otlp_http/acme        # exactly one item
    count: 1
  - exporter: otlp_grpc/globex      # never reaches it
    count: 0
```

| Assertion | Passes when |
| --- | --- |
| `exporter: E` | at least one item reaches `E` |
| `exporter: E` with `count: N` | exactly `N` items reach `E` over the whole window and shutdown |
| `exporter: E` with `count: 0` | `E` receives nothing |

Items are spans, log records or metric data points. With `count`, items of every signal that `E` received
are summed. Empty export requests, such as an idle pipeline's flush at shutdown, are ignored.

## Exact counts

`count` is exact, which makes it useful beyond routing:

- **Whole traces.** A three-span error trace kept by `tail_sampling` must give `count: 3`, not 1.
- **Aggregation.** Four data points summed into three series must give `count: 3`.
- **Fan-out.** The same record reaching two exporters gives `count: 1` at each.

A failure shows what each exporter received:

```text
✗ exporter otlp_http/backend receives 2 item(s)
  Expected: 2 spans
  Actual:   1 span
  Exporter: otlp_http/backend
  Received: otlp_http/backend: 1 span
```

The wording follows the signal the exporter actually received. A trace fixture that feeds a `span_metrics`
connector is reported in data points at the metrics exporter.

## "Never reaches"

`count: 0` is the way to assert that one exporter received nothing while others may have. Because `count` waits
for the full timeout, "never" is observed rather than assumed. To assert that **no** exporter received anything,
use [`dropped: true`](/docs/assertions/dropped).

## Timing

A test containing any `count` assertion observes the full timeout. A test whose only delivery assertion is
`exporter: E`, without `count`, can finish early once it holds and output has settled for 500 ms.

Connectors that emit on a timer, such as `span_metrics` and `service_graph`, emit again on every flush interval.
Avoid `count` on their output, because the number depends on timing. Assert presence and attributes instead.

## Rules

- `count` needs `exporter:` in the same assertion.
- `count` can't be combined with `attribute:` or `dropped:`. Write separate assertions.
- The exporter must be used by a pipeline. A connector ID is rejected: assert on the exporters of the pipelines
  it feeds.
