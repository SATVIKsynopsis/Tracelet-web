---
title: Assertion limitations
description: What the assertion engine can't check, and how to test around it.
icon: TriangleAlert
---

Assertions cover **attributes** and **delivery** (where items go, and how many). Everything below is outside
what Tracelet v0.1.0 can assert.

## Fields that can't be asserted

| Signal | Not assertable |
| --- | --- |
| Traces | span name, kind, status, events, links, timing |
| Logs | body, severity number and text, trace context |
| Metrics | metric name, description, unit, type, data point values, temporality |

These fields still reach the Collector and drive your processors: a filter on `severity_number`, a sampling
policy on `status.code`, or a `transform` on the body. You can test their **effect** on attributes and delivery,
but not the fields themselves.

Ways to work around this:

- **Status or severity filters:** assert `dropped: true` or a `count` on the alerting exporter.
- **Body parsing:** assert on the attributes the parser produced, as the [log redaction example](/docs/examples/log-redaction) does.
- **Aggregation:** assert the resulting data-point `count`, and that dropped labels are `absent`.
- **Metric renames:** not testable today.

## Value checks

- `equals` must hold for **every** item. There is no "one of" check. An attribute that differs between items
  (status `200` on one data point and `500` on another) can only be checked with `present` or `matches`.
- There is no way to assert an attribute's type without its exact value.
- `matches` works on strings only.
- Only scalar values can be compared. Arrays, maps and bytes can't be matched with `equals`.

## Timing-dependent output

- Connectors that emit on a timer (`span_metrics`, `service_graph`) need `metrics_flush_interval` below the test
  timeout, and emit repeatedly. Avoid `count` on their output.
- `groupbytrace` discards pending traces at shutdown, so `dropped` can pass for the wrong reason when its
  `wait_duration` isn't shorter than the timeout. Tracelet warns.

## Stateful processors

One test injects one fixture into a fresh Collector. Processors whose behavior depends on several requests over
time, such as `cumulative_to_delta`, rate limiting or probabilistic sampling rates, can't be exercised
meaningfully. A fixture can still hold many items, such as a whole trace or several data points, so per-request
behavior is testable.

## Not observable by design

- Anything the receiver would have done, because receivers are replaced.
- Anything the exporter or backend would have done, because exporters are replaced and no backend is contacted.

See [Known limitations](/docs/advanced/limitations) for limitations outside the assertion engine.
